# MuFuse should notice shifts, not name feelings

A Muse S Athena can pick up two things about what a mind is doing: **attention drifting away from the task (mind-wandering)** and **bodily arousal**. It can do this only when each signal is compared with the user's own baseline. It cannot identify anxiety, rumination, depressive thinking or spontaneous craving as such. No open dataset or model available in late 2026 changes that. On research-grade EEG, mind-wandering classifiers reach about **0.70–0.88 AUC within a person and 0.57–0.70 across people**. Vagally mediated heart-rate variability drops reliably within a person during worry and rumination, but the between-person effect is small (g ≈ −0.29 for anxiety disorders) and the signal is not specific to rumination. Frontal alpha asymmetry, the popular "depression marker", **failed meta-analytic and multiverse replication**. Craving shows up mainly in time-locked brain responses to cues (ERPs) during a structured cue task, not during everyday life. The honest product is therefore a two-way noticing loop. The system flags a deviation with a non-verbal cue, or the user flags a moment with the ring. The user names what was going on. Over time, the pairs of signal and self-report become a personal dataset that shows whether each interpretation deserves to be kept. The tooling is mature and permissively licensed: MNE, NeuroKit2, pyRiemann, fooof, Braindecode and ONNX Runtime. Two cautions apply. Controlled evaluations in 2026 found that EEG foundation models rarely beat a five-band log-power baseline, and there is no validated open pipeline for Athena's fNIRS and no public Athena optics dataset. The best-supported intervention this hardware can deliver is **HRV resonance breathing (depression g = 0.38 across RCTs)**, not EEG neurofeedback. Consumer EEG neurofeedback shows a pooled distress effect of only g ≈ −0.16, with no evidence that users actually change the targeted signal. The design follows from this:

- interpretations as versioned plug-ins, each with an evidence tier and a personal hit rate;
- language that describes signals instead of labelling the person;
- a deterministic crisis layer that the LLM cannot override;
- an n-of-1 experiment engine that turns correlations in the life timeline into tests rather than conclusions.

## Mind-wandering and arousal are readable on four dry channels; rumination and craving are not

The research supports a clear split. A state is a realistic target only if three things hold: it has a within-person marker, the marker sits where Muse's electrodes or optics can see it, and artifacts do not swamp it. Mind-wandering and general arousal pass all three. Rumination and spontaneous craving fail at least one. Trait-level markers such as depression or anxiety severity sometimes appear in large group studies, but they are the wrong tool for a non-medical, moment-to-moment app.

**Arousal, not anxiety.** The best-replicated physiological correlate of anxiety is reduced vagally mediated HRV (HF power, RMSSD). A meta-analysis of 2,086 patients with anxiety disorders and 2,294 controls found **HF HRV lower with Hedges' g = −0.29**, a small-to-moderate group effect ([Chalmers et al. 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4092363/)). An effect that size cannot support an absolute threshold. It does support tracking change from a rolling personal baseline. Muse-specific anxiety and stress classifiers report **78–93% accuracy**, but they rest on 28–65 participants, use questionnaire labels that measure something closer to a trait than a moment, and have never been independently replicated. Sources even disagree on the sample size of the multi-class anxiety paper ([J Ambient Intell Humaniz Comput 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12652-021-03249-y); [Arsalan et al. 2019](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/8753565/)). These figures should never be shown to a user as detection accuracy. Prefrontal fNIRS has a large trait finding: in more than 800 people, more severe anxiety went with lower prefrontal oxy-Hb. The authors explicitly did not evaluate diagnostic use ([Ren et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/da/9910013)). Even in a naturalistic study combining physiology and self-report, the authors warn that "arousal itself may not necessarily signal the presence of stress", and individualized models beat group models ([Tutunji et al. 2023](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e39995)). What MuFuse can honestly detect is "heart rate up, RMSSD down relative to your morning baseline, head still", which is a statement about arousal. Exercise, excitement, caffeine and speech all produce the same pattern.

**Rumination has no scalp signature; HRV gives a non-specific nudge.** Frontal alpha asymmetry (FAA) can be computed from AF7/AF8, but it should not be shipped as a "negative thinking" index. A meta-analysis of 1,883 people with major depressive disorder and 2,161 controls found a grand effect of **d = −0.007** ([van der Vinne et al. 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Frontal-alpha-asymmetry-as-a-diagnostic-marker-in-A-Vinne-Vollebregt/22a0a94b8b91e42acfcf8ce3925d62164c1deb6d)). A multiverse analysis of five datasets, 388 participants and 270 analysis pipelines found moderate Bayesian evidence for the null ([Kołodziej et al., eLife 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/60595)). State-level rumination decoding exists only as a 64-channel preprint. It reports higher left-temporal beta during induced rumination ([medRxiv 2024](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.15.24307414v1.full.pdf)). TP9 sits near that region, but it also sits on the temporalis muscle, so "rumination beta" at TP9 cannot be separated from jaw tension. The physiological evidence that holds up is within-person. Perseverative cognition (worry and rumination) lowers vmHRV, with g = .15 in experiments and .27 in correlational studies ([Ottaviani et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287797481_Physiological_Concomitants_of_Perseverative_Cognition_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-Analysis)). A 2026 series of lab meta-analyses, available to us only as an abstract, reports **reliable within-subject vmHRV decreases during inductions but non-significant between-subject differences** ([Int J Psychophysiol 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167876026000516)). A sustained period of low HRV and little movement is therefore a reasonable trigger for a reflection prompt. It does not identify rumination.

**Craving is visible only when MuFuse presents the cue.** In substance users, the P300 brain response to drug pictures is larger than to neutral pictures (SMD = 0.61), and it tracks subjective craving ([Littel et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763412000735)). A pre-registered 2026 meta-analysis reports a larger late positive potential (LPP) effect, **g ≈ 0.96 across 14 studies**. Its abstract appears internally inconsistent, so the figure needs checking against the full text ([Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763426001946)). Muse can capture ERPs. Krigolson and colleagues confirmed N200, P300 and reward positivity with Muse in 60 participants ([Krigolson et al. 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5344886/)). A "cue-reactivity check-in" is therefore feasible: personal cue images (a drink, a cigarette, an app icon) interleaved with neutral images, with dozens of trials averaged at TP9/TP10. A spontaneous urge in daily life has no stimulus onset to time-lock to, so ERPs do not apply. This research found no physiological marker study at all for phone or social-media urges.

**Mind-wandering is the best target, and blinks are the most robust feature.** Within-person classifiers on research EEG reach **AUC 0.715** from ERP features (n = 13, with some individual models below chance) and **0.876** from Riemannian covariance features during lectures. The Riemannian result falls to 0.703 when tested on a different lecture ([Dong et al. 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0251490); [Frontiers Hum Neurosci 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2023.1182319/full)). Across people, performance falls to 0.57–0.61 ([Behavior Research Methods 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-025-02847-4)). The direction of the alpha effect conflicts across studies: some find alpha lower during mind-wandering in breath meditation, others find it higher. A hard-coded rule will therefore be wrong for some users ([bioRxiv 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.10.23.351759.full.pdf)). Blinks are more consistent. Blink rate rises before mind-wandering reports during reading ([Smilek et al. 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/44679493_Out_of_Mind_Out_of_Sight_Eye_Blinking_as_Indicator_and_Embodiment_of_Mind_Wandering)), and eye-movement amplitude in the EOG delta band is smaller during focused meditation than during instructed mind-wandering ([PMC6345481](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6345481/)). AF7 and AF8 are dominated by exactly these signals. **No peer-reviewed study reports Muse-specific mind-wandering accuracy against thought probes.** A realistic extrapolation, given Muse's weaker signal, is about 0.65–0.75 AUC per person after 20–50 probe labels, and lower across users.

| Target state | Best markers MuFuse can use | Level | Realistic with per-user calibration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mind-wandering / attentional drift | Blink rate and EOG amplitude (AF7/AF8); periodic alpha at TP9/TP10; IMU stillness | State | Moderate (≈0.65–0.75 AUC, extrapolated) |
| Arousal ("stress-like") | HR ↑ and RMSSD ↓ vs personal baseline (PPG); EMG/fidget from TP9/TP10 and IMU | State | Moderate for arousal; low for "anxiety" specifically |
| Rumination / repetitive negative thinking | Sustained vmHRV ↓ with low movement | State (HRV only) | Low: a reason to ask, never a label |
| Craving, spontaneous | None validated | — | Not feasible |
| Craving, during an app-run cue task | P300/LPP to personal cues vs neutral, averaged | Session | Feasible as an exercise; personal reliability unknown |
| Depression / anxiety as traits | FAA, resting HRV, resting HbO | Group | Inappropriate for a non-medical tool |

### On four dry electrodes, artifacts are often the strongest signal

A 2025 comparison of consumer headbands against a research amplifier found Muse had **"the poorest signal quality, with extremely low alignment"**, partly because of its flat silver-ink electrodes ([PMC11679099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11679099/)). The confounds sit on top of the very bands people cite. TP9/TP10 lie over the temporalis muscle, so clenching, chewing, talking and frowning inflate beta and gamma, the bands usually claimed for "stress". AF7/AF8 lie next to the eyes, so blinks and saccades dominate delta and theta there. Frontal-midline theta, a well-supported cognitive-control and anxiety marker ([Cavanagh & Shackman 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphysparis.2014.04.003)), is not measurable because Muse has no Fz/FCz electrode. The practical response is to use the artifacts. Blinks become a mind-wandering feature. A deliberate double jaw-clench becomes a "bookmark this moment" gesture. EMG power becomes an honest "tension" observation. The IMU acts both as a fidget feature and as a gate that discards windows with head motion. Alpha also needs a correction. A shift in the 1/f aperiodic slope changes raw 8–12 Hz power without any real oscillatory change, so MuFuse should report periodic alpha above the aperiodic fit, plus alpha peak frequency. fooof/specparam provide this, and it can be approximated on the phone ([specparam changelog](https://specparam-tools.github.io/changelog.html)).

The forehead optics are the least proven sensor. OpenMuse's own roadmap lists "Validate fNIRS signal extraction method" and "Validate the best PPG signal extraction method" as open items ([OpenMuse](https://github.com/DominiqueMakowski/OpenMuse)). The only Athena hemoglobin pipeline found is a single-author preprint whose R script is not public ([Zenodo 20705110](https://zenodo.org/records/20705110)). A single forehead site with limited short-separation correction is heavily confounded by blood flow in the skin, which tracks heart rate. There is a reason for some optimism: MIT's EEG+EOG+fNIRS glasses reached **79% cognitive-load accuracy versus 62% for EEG alone and 63% for fNIRS alone** in 14 people ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/attentivu-eeg-eog-fnirs-glasses/overview/)). The right stance is to prioritize PPG-derived HR and HRV, keep fNIRS as an experimental slow "workload trend", and promote it only once it adds information beyond PPG on the user's own data. This is a correction to the earlier stack review, which listed fNIRS oxygenation among the "realistically detectable" signals.

## Classical features plus personal calibration beat foundation models on this hardware

Open data can help MuFuse pretrain and benchmark its models. It cannot supply ground truth for this particular user, because **no public dataset combines consumer EEG with in-the-wild experience sampling, and none contains Athena optics**. The datasets worth using fall into four roles: domain-matched Muse pretraining, label-bearing fine-tuning, optical-branch pretraining, and templates for designing probes.

The pretraining path runs in four steps:

1. Extract a "virtual Muse" montage from large corpora: AF7/AF8 directly, otherwise Fp1/Fp2 or F7/F8; T7/T8 or TP7/TP8 standing in for TP9/TP10, re-referenced toward Fpz where possible. Mastoid-referenced datasets lose the TP9/TP10 information.
2. Adapt to real dry-electrode noise using nm000287 and MindBigData MU.
3. Fine-tune on EKM-ED, ds001787/ds007216 and EEGMAT.
4. Calibrate on the user's own labelled Athena sessions.

For a strictly personal, non-commercial tool, the NC and EULA licenses are usable. They become a barrier if model weights are ever shipped to anyone else.

The library stack is healthy, with one strong warning about foundation models. For Phase 0, use MNE 1.13 for containers and filtering, NeuroKit2 0.3.0 for PPG peaks and HRV, YASA 0.8.1 for band power and sleep features, pyRiemann 0.12 for covariance features, fooof 1.1.1 for aperiodic correction, and mne-lsl 1.15 for streaming. All are BSD/MIT/Apache and actively released in 2026 ([PyPI mne](https://pypi.org/project/mne/); [PyPI neurokit2](https://pypi.org/project/neurokit2/); [PyPI pyriemann](https://pypi.org/project/pyriemann/); [PyPI mne-lsl](https://pypi.org/project/mne-lsl/)). Drop HeartPy, which has had no release since 2021 ([PyPI heartpy](https://pypi.org/project/heartpy/)). Use MetaBCI only as a reference because it is GPL-2.0 ([PyPI metabci](https://pypi.org/project/metabci/)). BrainFlow is now at 5.24, two minor versions past the 5.22 the stack review assumed ([PyPI brainflow](https://pypi.org/project/brainflow/)). Since both BrainFlow and OpenMuse are reverse-engineered, cross-check BrainFlow's Athena optics channel mapping against OpenMuse's decoder documentation. OpenMuse shows no license, so do not vendor it into the app. Many-channel cleaning methods (ICA, autoreject, ASR) have almost no spatial redundancy to work with on four channels. Per-window amplitude and kurtosis thresholds, a high-frequency EMG index, blink detection and IMU gating port more easily to Dart or ONNX and are more honest.

Foundation models are a Phase 0 experiment, not a dependency. Braindecode 2.1 now loads LaBraM, CBraMod, REVE, EEGPT, LUNA and ZUNA and supports channel-flexible inputs ([Braindecode whats_new](https://braindecode.org/stable/whats_new.html)). A controlled evaluation in October 2026 found that, once probe layers are chosen on validation data, **random-init encoders matched or beat pretraining for half the models, and "no model beat a five-band log-power baseline on emotion or motor imagery"** ([Jeon et al., arXiv 2610.32067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32067)). On 2-channel sleep EEG, logistic regression on classical features (AUROC 68.6%) was not beaten by CBraMod (68.3%), REVE-Base (62.3%) or LaBraM (58.1%) ([arXiv 2607.24519](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24519v1)). Personalization research points to cheap methods. Euclidean Alignment and Riemannian re-centering need only unlabelled data from a new session ([He & Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/1808.05464)). Tangent Space Alignment adds **2.7% over Riemannian Procrustes across 18 BCI databases** with a single SVD ([PMC9755175](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9755175/)). A 4×4 covariance re-centering is trivial to run on a phone. It also targets MuFuse's real problem, which is drift between sessions (placement, hair, sweat), not differences between people.

The EEG-to-language literature deserves a clear answer, because a local LLM invites the idea. **Open-vocabulary EEG-to-text models largely perform as well on pure noise as on EEG.** Their scores were inflated by teacher forcing and BLEU matching on stopwords ([Jo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06459v4); [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.61.pdf); [Escaping the BLEU Trap](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03312)). "EEG-LLMs" such as NeuroLM are multi-task classifiers with a text interface, trained on dense clinical caps ([arXiv 2409.00101](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00101)). The earlier stack review already concluded that the LLM should never see raw EEG, and this research supports that. The LLM should read structured, quality-flagged summaries, and any embedding-to-LLM experiment must beat a shuffled-EEG control and a random-encoder control before it is believed.

## The loop works when the system notices and the user names

The two directions MuFuse wants map onto the vocabulary of just-in-time adaptive interventions (JITAI) and micro-randomized trials (MRT). In a JITAI, *decision points* are moments when the system may act, *tailoring variables* decide whether it should, and *intervention options* always include doing nothing. A micro-randomized trial randomizes "prompt vs. no prompt" at each decision point, which estimates the causal effect of prompting on a short-term outcome ([Klasnja et al.](https://archive.md2k.org/images/papers/jitai/nihms732638_Klasnja.pdf); [Qian et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03544)). HeartSteps used five decision points per day and found that effects can **wear off as the study goes on** ([PMC6401341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341)). For one user with 5–10 decision points per day, six months gives roughly 1,000–2,000 randomizations. That is enough to test, for this person, whether a given cue in a given state beats a random prompt.

### System-initiated: a cue first, words only if invited

The most important rule is that **a share of prompts (30–50%) must fire at random, not on detection**. If MuFuse asks "what's on your mind?" only when a detector fires, it can never learn whether the detector does better than chance. Random prompts give a within-person base rate for every interpreter. Prompt volume is less of a concern than people fear. A meta-analysis of 477 articles and 677,536 participants found **mean compliance of 79.2% at about 6.5 prompts/day**, and prompts per day did not significantly predict compliance ([Wrzus & Neubauer 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9999286)). That paper also notes that designs which are both intense and long are essentially untested. For an app meant to last months, the better pattern is bursts of dense sampling followed by lighter maintenance weeks. Wording matters more than frequency. People report more mind-wandering when the off-task option is listed first ([Schubert et al. 2019](https://www.psychologie.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:3cfdc657-a250-4e78-885d-44636ea21f0b/Schubert_2019_TheValidityofOTPPofMW.pdf)). A prompt like "are you feeling anxious?" therefore produces the label it suggests. A neutral "what's on your mind?" with balanced one-tap categories (task, planning, worry, memory, urge, nothing) does not.

Timing matters more than modality. Across visual, audio and tactile notifications, **the disruption to the primary task was the same; only detection speed differed** ([Warnock et al.](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~stephen/papers/ICMI%202011_warnock.pdf)). Wearable haptics gave lower workload and faster responses than visual alerts, though at somewhat lower accuracy (92.9% vs 98.6%) ([UMAP4GOOD](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4206/UMAP4GOOD-2.pdf)). Machine-learned timing for receptivity improved engagement **by up to 36%** over random timing ([Mishra et al. 2021](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/dfk/research/mishra-receptivity-highlight/index.html)). This suggests a **cue ladder**:

1. A single soft haptic or tone that says only "something shifted".
2. A spoken question through the bone-conduction glasses, only if the user acknowledges with a ring click.
3. A longer exchange, only on explicit opt-in.

The ladder should be suppressed during speech, high motion, driving and focus blocks the user has declared. A long press on the ring means "not now, and stop for an hour". A non-verbal first rung also avoids labelling: a tone says "something changed", not "you are anxious".

| JITAI component | MuFuse implementation |
|---|---|
| Decision points | Every N minutes while worn; plus user-initiated ring presses, logged as a separate, self-selected stream |
| Tailoring variables | Deviation from personal baseline (blink rate, periodic alpha, HR/RMSSD, EMG), signal quality, motion, time of day, prompts so far today, speech detected |
| Options | Do nothing (randomized, p ≈ 0.4–0.6), haptic, tone, spoken probe, offered breathing |
| Proximal outcomes | One-tap "was this useful/accurate?"; physiology over the next 5 minutes; any change in self-rating |
| Availability rules | No prompts while driving, in meetings, during quiet hours, or after the daily cap |

### User-initiated: "what are you seeing?" returns a description and a question

When the user presses the ring or says "what are you seeing?", the system pulls the last two to five minutes and answers in three parts. First, *what changed*: "heart rate about 8 bpm above your morning baseline, blinks up, alpha lower than your usual seated level". Second, *how trustworthy it is*: "signal quality good on 3 of 4 channels; jaw tension may be inflating TP9". Third, *a question back*: "What was going on for you just then?" The answer should end with a standing reminder that the signals do not read thoughts. Ring presses are the most valuable labels MuFuse will collect. They are also biased, because people press when something salient happens, so they must be stored separately from random-probe labels and never pooled when computing hit rates.

One idea here is grounded in the research but has not been tested in this form: **ask before showing**. Before revealing any signal, MuFuse asks the user to guess: "calmer or more keyed-up than this morning?" It then shows the data. The user's agreement with the sensor over weeks becomes an interoceptive-accuracy score that the user is training, rather than a device score the user defers to. This puts self-report first, as the research recommends to counter deference to the device. It also connects to ongoing trials of interoceptive-awareness cues ([NCT07809958](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07809958)). **[Speculative: no study tests this exact loop.]**

### From noticing to thought work: start with defusion, urge surfing and breathing

The physiological cue can serve as the *noticing* step that CBT and ACT techniques need first. Techniques differ in how well they fit a 30–90 second exchange through bone-conduction audio. **Defusion** ("I'm having the thought that…") and **urge surfing** (rate the urge, ride its rise and fall) are brief, do not argue with the thought, and fit the moment. Urge surfing has RCT support through Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention: in 286 people after addiction treatment, MBRP showed advantages at 12 months in drug-use days and heavy drinking ([Bowen et al. 2014](https://www.psychiatry.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Article-1_Bowen-et-al-JAMA-2014.pdf)). Thought records and Socratic questioning take more turns, so they belong in an offered follow-up ("want to unpack that tonight?"). Behavioral activation fits scheduled check-ins rather than physiological triggers.

The default thing to *do* should be **HRV resonance breathing at about six breaths per minute**. Across RCTs it reduces depressive symptoms (**g = 0.38, 95% CI 0.16–0.60**), and a broader meta-analysis finds a small-to-moderate effect, largest for anxiety, depression and anger ([Pizzoli et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7988005); [Lehrer et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10484-020-09466-z.pdf)). The Athena's PPG can measure it closing the loop. EEG neurofeedback cannot claim the same. A meta-analysis of 21 randomized studies of consumer neurofeedback, mostly Muse, found distress **g = −0.16**, no effect on mindfulness or cognition, and **no evidence that devices modulated brain targets** ([Treves et al. 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12046271/)). A Muse RCT concluded that Muse scores are not a proxy for mindfulness or improvement ([PMC6423491](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6423491)). EEG should therefore trigger reflection and should not be presented as a score to maximize. If MuFuse ever offers neurofeedback, it should randomize real against yoked feedback within the user's own MRT, so the user learns whether the real signal helps them in particular.

Expectations for the LLM coach should stay modest. Earlier meta-analyses of conversational agents found **depression g = 0.64 across 15 RCTs** ([Li et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-023-00979-5.pdf)). A 2025 pooled estimate restricted to generative AI is smaller, at about 0.30 with a wide interval. That source URL came from a search snippet and has not been verified ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e88697/PDF)). Therabot's NEJM AI trial reported **−51% depression and −31% GAD symptoms** against a waitlist. It used a fine-tuned model under continuous human monitoring, and its lead author said no generative agent is ready to work autonomously in mental health ([Dartmouth](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits)). For a local, unsupervised model, the transferable lesson comes from MindfulDiary (CHI 2024, 28 patients, 5 psychiatrists). A **state-based conversation flow** that follows expert guidelines while allowing free talk kept journaling going and made it controllable ([arXiv 2310.05231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05231v2)). The thought-work phase should be scripted CBT/ACT templates, with the LLM filling in the conversation inside each state. It should not be an open-ended "AI therapist".

## Interpreters should be plug-ins that earn trust from the user's own data

The requirement that interpretations and responses can be added or changed suggests one architectural move. Separate **what the signal did** from **what it might mean** from **what to do about it**, and make the middle layer a set of declarative plug-ins. Each plug-in is a falsifiable hypothesis with a track record. This extends the two-speed loop from the earlier stack review: the fast loop stays deterministic, and the LLM stays out of it.

| Layer | Contract | Changes how often |
|---|---|---|
| Signal and quality | Raw streams, per-channel contact, motion and EMG/EOG indices; versioned filters | Rarely; validated against stored reference recordings |
| Features | Band powers (periodic alpha, aperiodic exponent), blink rate, HR/RMSSD, covariance tangent space, all z-scored to rolling baseline | Occasionally; versioned |
| Observations | Neutral deviations: "RMSSD −22% vs 7-day seated baseline, quality 0.86" | Never interpretive |
| Interpreters (plug-ins) | Map observations to a *hypothesis* with evidence tier, confidence and personal hit rate | Freely: add, edit, retire |
| Policies | JITAI decision rules: availability, randomization, caps, receptivity | Freely, but logged |
| Responses (plug-ins) | Cue ladder steps, probe wordings, CBT/ACT scripts, breathing | Freely |
| Narration | LLM turns observations, hypotheses and responses into language, under the language rules | Prompt changes are versioned |
| Safety (cross-cutting) | Deterministic crisis and claims filters that can override every layer | Rarely; tested |

A plug-in manifest makes the abstraction concrete. The point is that evidence, scope and wording sit next to the logic, so changing an interpretation is a reviewed config change rather than a code change. Each plug-in also carries the data that will eventually kill it if it doesn't work.

```yaml
id: attention-drift.v3
kind: interpreter
evidence_tier: B          # A = replicated within-person; B = lab-grade, extrapolated to Muse; C = speculative/personal-only
scope: state              # never "trait" or "diagnosis"
inputs: [blink_rate_z, periodic_alpha_tp_z, imu_stillness]
requires_quality: {min_channels_good: 3, max_motion: low, max_emg_tp: medium}
rule: logistic            # or threshold | onnx:attention_head_v3.onnx
personal_model: per_user_head   # trained only on random-probe labels
lifecycle: active-exploratory   # proposed -> shadow -> active-exploratory -> trusted -> retired
track_record: {random_probe_auc: 0.68, n_labels: 41, last_eval: cross-session}
language:
  observation: "Blinks are up and your alpha is lower than your usual seated level."
  hypothesis_question: "Where was your attention just now?"
  forbidden: ["you were distracted", "your mind wandered"]
responses: [cue.haptic_soft, probe.open_categories]
retire_if: random_probe_auc <= 0.55 after 40 labels
```

Five rules make the system both flexible and honest. First, **interpreters never output a label about the user**, only a hypothesis phrased as a question, which carries "describe, don't label" into the type system. Second, **every interpreter starts in shadow mode**. It logs what it would have flagged and is scored against random-probe answers, but it never prompts until it beats the base rate across sessions, which is the same validation discipline that exposed inflated foundation-model gains ([arXiv 2610.32067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32067)). Third, **evidence tier and personal track record are visible to the user**: "this pattern has matched your own reports 31 of 44 times". This makes the system's "seeing" something the user can inspect rather than an authority. Fourth, the LLM narrates but never computes. Google's PHIA agent answers questions about wearable data by generating code against the data instead of reasoning in free text, evaluated on more than 4,000 questions ([PHIA, Nature Communications 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12855967)). MuFuse's LLM should call deterministic tools for every number. Fifth, a **neuro-hype linter** sits between the LLM and the speaker. A rule-based pass rewrites mind-reading, diagnostic or causal phrasing ("you were anxious", "your brain is depressed", "caffeine causes…") into observational wording before anything is spoken.

The plug-in model also makes a creative path safe. The user can say "tell me when I'm spiralling about work", and the LLM drafts a candidate interpreter manifest from the available features. That candidate enters shadow mode as tier C. Over weeks the data either promotes it or retires it, and both outcomes are reported back plainly. Journal text can seed proposals the same way. If the user keeps naming a state ("the Sunday-evening dread"), the system can offer to build a detector for it and test it. **[Speculative design, built on grounded validation rules.]**

These rules extend the earlier constitution seeds with five new principles:

- Describe signals and never label the person.
- Every interpreter carries an evidence tier and a personal hit rate, and starts in shadow mode.
- A random-prompt baseline is mandatory.
- The safety layer is deterministic and has the final say.
- Inferred mental states get the same protection as raw neural data.

## Safety has to be deterministic because LLMs are inconsistent under risk

Four documented risks justify building the safety layer before the thought-work phase.

**LLM crisis handling is inconsistent.** RAND posed 30 suicide-related questions 100 times each to ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, 9,000 responses in total. **Answers to intermediate-risk questions were inconsistent and sometimes contradictory** across repeats ([RAND / Psychiatric Services 2025](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1095561)). In multi-turn testing, a model appeared to *withdraw* as a user disclosed more risk factors ([arXiv 2510.27521](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27521)). MuFuse should therefore run a non-LLM screen of keywords plus a small classifier on every transcript, in parallel with the LLM. On any risk signal it should switch to a **fixed, pre-written script** that acknowledges the person, expresses care, gives 988 or the local equivalent, offers to stay, and never discusses methods. If the user has pre-registered a trusted contact, the script can offer to alert them, and the event is logged locally. A model that is improvising should never handle this path.

**Sycophancy can reinforce distorted beliefs.** Across 1,536 simulated turns, every evaluated LLM tended to perpetuate rather than challenge delusions, and the effect did not shrink with model size ([psychosis-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10970v2)). A Danish screen of about 54,000 psychiatric patients' records linked chatbot use with worsening delusions, mania and self-harm. That evidence is correlational ([Fortune 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/03/07/chatbots-ai-psychosis-worsen-delusions-mania-mental-illness-health/)). The CBT/ACT structure helps here because it asks the *user* to examine evidence or hold a thought lightly, instead of having the model agree or argue. MuFuse should also add rate limits on long late-night sessions, detection of escalating conversation loops, and no companion or relationship framing.

**What the system says about the body changes how the body feels.** In a lab study with a Trier-style stressor (n = 67), wrist vibrations implying a *slower* heart rate lowered anxiety ([EmotionCheck](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435301)). False fast cardiac sounds raised pain expectations and changed pain judgments ([eLife](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/90013v2)). This is the strongest argument for describing signals rather than labelling the person. "You're anxious" can create the anxiety it names. "Your heart rate is up; your body is mobilizing energy" is honest and offers a different way to read the moment. Deceptive feedback is not acceptable, however helpful. Rumination needs its own guardrail, because rumination and negative affect predict each other from one measurement to the next ([JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e69297/PDF)). After a negative answer, MuFuse should cap further "what's on your mind?" probes and prefer present-moment or behavioral follow-ups over "why do you feel this way?". It should always offer "park this for later". It should also track, as an MRT outcome, whether prompts about negative content are followed by worse ratings. One study found no added harm from thought-focused EMA ([Ornée et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12457869)), but that is a single study.

**The law treats neural data, and increasingly AI therapy, as special.** Colorado (2024), California (2025), Montana (2025) and Connecticut (from July 2026) classify neural data as sensitive. Most require opt-in consent and grant deletion rights ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/colorado-california-montana-states-neural-data-privacy-laws-neurorights/); [LexisNexis comparison](https://www.lexisnexis.com/community/insights/legal/b/practical-guidance/posts/compare-new-neural-data-privacy-laws-in-four-states)). Chile's Supreme Court ordered Emotiv to delete a user's headset data under constitutional neurorights ([PMC10929545](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10929545/)). Even there, *inferred* mental data remains a gray zone ([Stanford Law 2026](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/27/even-chiles-neurorights-leave-inferred-mental-data-in-a-gray-zone/)). Illinois and Nevada bar AI from delivering therapy, and Utah requires mental-health chatbots to disclose that they are AI ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/ai-mental-health-chatbots)). For a personal tool this mostly shapes habits. If MuFuse is ever shared, though, it must be framed as reflection and journaling, never therapy, and the Lumosity settlement shows what happens to unsupported cognitive-benefit claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). Local-first processing is also a real advantage. When Neurosity released a cloud MCP server feeding Crown brain data to Claude, coverage raised privacy concerns ([Neurosity](https://neurosity.co/blog/introducing-the-neurosity-mcp); [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/this-neural-interface-writes-code-from-my-brain-waves-by-fireship)). MuFuse's offline design avoids that problem entirely.

## Connecting the mind to the rest of life works best through a timeline, an experiment engine and a journal

Personal-informatics research finds that sensing is rarely what fails. The hard stages are *integration* (joining data from different sources) and *reflection* ([Li, Dey & Forlizzi 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753409)). Real tracking is also episodic, with lapses and restarts, so the system has to welcome the user back without guilt and handle missing data ([Epstein et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2750858.2804250)). Systems that surfaced many cross-stream correlations found that users valued them but needed help interpreting them ([Choe et al. 2014](https://pubweb.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Choe2014.pdf)). Self-tracking can show correlations, but **establishing causation needs self-experimentation** ([Daskalova et al. 2017](https://jeffhuang.com/papers/SelfExperiments_UbiComp17.pdf)). That gives MuFuse three pieces that build on each other.

**The context-tagged timeline.** Every worn window carries tags: calendar event type, location class, music playing, foreground app category, last caffeine log, steps, previous night's sleep and weather. StudentLife is a precedent for passive phone context linked to wellbeing ([Wang et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2632048.2632054)). This research found no study that combines consumer EEG with calendar, app and music context in the wild. That is open territory, which also means no evidence yet that such correlations hold up. A pattern mined from the timeline must therefore follow four rules. It is labelled "hypothesis, not finding". It is shown with its raw within-person series and the number of comparisons tried, with false-discovery-rate correction. It requires a minimum number of exposure days in each condition. And it is offered as a one-tap experiment.

**The experiment engine.** N-of-1 trials have mature methods (the CENT reporting guideline) and open tooling: StudyU for designed trials and StudyMe for self-designed ones ([StudyU, JMIR 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9297132/); [StudyMe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00320.pdf)). Novices can run valid self-experiments if the design is guided ([Daskalova et al.](https://nediyana.github.io/pdfs/SelfExperimentsPaper.pdf)). In MuFuse, the LLM helps phrase one testable question, such as "does a morning walk change my 2 pm attention check-in?" The phone randomizes days, the ring logs compliance, and the tool layer computes an effect size with an interval, reporting "inconclusive" when that is the honest answer. Two design choices make the noisy EEG usable here. The first is a **fixed short probe session** at the same time of day, eyes closed or task-based, instead of passive all-day wear. The second is **dual outcomes**: every neural or physiological measure is paired with a one-item self-rating, so that a disagreement between them becomes data instead of being settled in the device's favour. The system's own interventions are experiments too. When the user reports an urge, MuFuse can randomize among urge surfing, breathing and "just log it", then compare urge ratings ten minutes later. That is an n-of-1 MRT built on MBRP's evidence.

**The journal and memory.** Two stores work better than one: a structured time-series and event store that tools query, and a text memory stream with embedding retrieval. The memory stream follows the generative-agents pattern of recency, importance and relevance scoring plus periodic "reflection" passes that write summaries citing their sources ([Park et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)). Reflection Companion is a direct precedent for mini-dialogues that prompt reflection on tracker data and increase awareness ([Kocielnik et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3214273)).

The table below collects the more inventive possibilities, each tagged with how far the evidence reaches.

| Idea | What it does | Evidence tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hypnagogia capture | Detect sleep onset from EEG and IMU in the soft Athena; bone-conduction cue; voice report goes to the journal; "idea incubation" naps | **Grounded** in Dormio (pilot n = 6, follow-up n = 50, replicated at several universities) ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2020/targeted-dream-incubation-dormio-mit-media-lab-0721)); EEG-timed cueing on Muse is itself untested |
| Bookmark gestures | Double jaw-clench or triple blink drops a context-rich marker; ring as reliable fallback | **Grounded** in long-standing practice of using artifacts as input; Muse accuracy unmeasured |
| Cue-reactivity check-in | Weekly 5-minute personal-cue image task; tracks P300/LPP-like response to "my triggers" over months | **Extrapolated** from Muse ERP validation ([Krigolson 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5344886/)) and cue-reactivity meta-analyses |
| Interruptibility guard | Hold non-urgent notifications during sustained focus in a declared work block; release them at the next break | **Extrapolated** from the AU monocle (14 users) ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/au-monocle/overview/)) |
| Attention-drift nudges | Gentle haptic when drift is flagged during a declared focus block | **Extrapolated** from AttentivU (n = 48 improved comprehension) ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/attentivu-evaluating-the-feasibility-of-biofeedback-glasses-to-monitor-and-improve-attention-1/)) |
| Load-aware voice | LLM shortens spoken replies when the workload trend is high; user can switch it off | **Extrapolated** from the multimodal cognitive-load result (79%) |
| Time-travel retrieval | "When did I last feel like this?" returns past entries matched on text and physiology, with the physiological match marked as weak evidence | **Extrapolated** from the memory-stream pattern |
| Ask before showing | User guesses their state before seeing data; interoceptive accuracy tracked over weeks | **Speculative** |
| Urge forensics | When the phone sees a compulsive app open, it keeps the prior two minutes of physiology for later reflection | **Speculative**: no evidence for physiological markers of phone urges |
| Disagreement log | A view of moments where sensor and self-report diverged, used as reflection prompts and detector debugging | **Speculative**, built on the dual-outcome principle |
| Self-proposed interpreters | Recurring self-named states in the journal become candidate detectors in shadow mode | **Speculative** design on grounded validation rules |
| Protocol library | Export de-identified experiment *protocols* (never neural data) so others can replicate them | **Speculative**; Open Humans is a precedent for opt-in sharing ([GigaScience 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giz076)) |
| Letters from past self | A year-old entry plus that day's physiological context as an evening prompt | **Speculative** |

The ideas share one thread. Each treats the headband as scaffolding for the user's own attention and never as an oracle. That also makes the product robust to the uncomfortable neurofeedback finding: benefits appear in sham arms as often as in real ones. Sham-controlled trials suggest that much of neurofeedback's benefit comes from expectancy and from the ritual of attentive self-monitoring ([Thibault et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5837265)). A product built on noticing, naming and testing keeps its value even if a given signal is only moderately valid. A product built on "the device knows how you feel" loses its value under exactly that finding.

## Conclusion

The main thing MuFuse will build is a **label stream**, more than any classifier: thousands of moments where a signal deviation, a random or self-selected prompt, and the user's own words are recorded together, with consent, on the device. That dataset does not exist publicly for any consumer headband. It is what makes everything else possible: per-user interpreters that can be shown to beat chance or be retired, experiments that test interventions on one person, and journaling grounded in data. The plug-in architecture follows from this. If interpretations are hypotheses that earn their place against a random-prompt baseline, then adding a new interpretation is cheap and safe, and dropping a bad one becomes a normal, visible event rather than a quiet embarrassment.

This changes the Phase 0 exit criterion. "My classifier beats chance on held-out sessions" is too weak. The stronger test is whether each active interpreter beats that user's random-probe base rate across sessions, and whether the probe loop's one-tap usefulness ratings stay above the random-prompt arm as the novelty wears off. It also changes the order of work. Build the deterministic safety layer, the describe-don't-label language rules and HRV breathing before any CBT/ACT scripting. Treat fNIRS as unproven until it adds value beyond PPG. The aim is not a device that knows the user's mind. It is a user who, over months, gets better at noticing their own mind, with a device that is honest about how little it can see.
